MitiBar keeps today's BS date in the tray, with a month grid, tithi, public holidays, a converter and
settings one click away. On Windows a small floating bar shows the date and time wherever you put it.
The popup opens from the tray, the bar or a global shortcut, Win+Shift+N, and closes when it loses
focus. It makes no network requests.

It is built on the [Rust library](/docs/rust/) and lives in its own repository,
[sanamhub/mitibar](https://github.com/sanamhub/mitibar), with its own version line.

## What it does

- **Tray**: today's BS day on the icon on Windows and Linux, and the day and month as the menu-bar
  title on macOS (the next release shows the bar's top line there instead). The tooltip has the BS and AD dates. The menu has Open, Copy today (BS), Copy
  today (AD), Show bar (Windows), Settings and Quit.
- **Floating bar (Windows)**: one or two lines you choose from weekday, time, a second clock, BS and
  AD dates, tithi and today's holiday. Drag it anywhere; it snaps to edges and stays above the
  taskbar.
- **Month view**: BS and AD day numbers, tithi under each day, holidays in red, events marked with a
  dot. Zoom out to a year or a decade, go to any date, or pin the popup open.
- **Settings**: language (English or Nepali), theme, the bar's two lines, the shortcut, a second
  time zone, and start at login.

## Build it yourself

```sh
cargo run -p mitibar --release
```

On Linux it needs a tray (StatusNotifierItem or the GNOME AppIndicator extension), fontconfig,
xkbcommon and D-Bus. The renderer is software only, so no OpenGL or GPU driver is needed.
