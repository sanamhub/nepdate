---
title: The Bikram Sambat calendar
description: The supported range, where month lengths come from, and what "today" means.
---

Bikram Sambat (BS, बिक्रम सम्बत्) is the official calendar of Nepal. It is a solar calendar about 56
years and 8 months ahead of the Gregorian one: BS 2081 began on 13 April 2024. A year has 12 months,
from Baishakh to Chaitra, and the new year falls in mid-April.

Months are numbered from 1 in every port, including TypeScript, where JavaScript's own `Date`
counts from 0.

## Month lengths come from data

A BS month has 29 to 32 days, and the lengths change from year to year. No formula gives them; they
are published in the official calendar. Every port reads them from one table in the hub's `data/`,
imported from C# NepDate, and never computes them.

Lengths beyond the officially published years are projections carried over from NepDate. When a new
calendar is published and a projected year changes, the hub updates the table and every port takes
it in a patch release ([Calendar data](/docs/project/data/)).

## The supported range

**BS 1901-01-01 to 2199-12-30**, which is AD 1844-04-11 to 2143-04-15. A date outside that range is
an error in every port, never a guess.

The patro data (tithi, holidays, events) covers less: BS 2077 to 2084
([Core and patro](/docs/concepts/core-and-patro/)).

## What "today" means

A BS date is a calendar day, not an instant. Which day it is depends on where you are, and Nepal is
at UTC+05:45. The ports that read the clock default to Nepal time, so the machine's own time zone
doesn't change the answer:

- TypeScript: `NepaliDate.today(timeZone = "Asia/Kathmandu")`.
- C++: `today(utc_offset)`, with +05:45 by default.
- C: `nepdate_today_nepal(unix_seconds)`, or `nepdate_from_unix` with any fixed offset. The caller
  passes the time, so the core never reads a clock.
- Python: `NepaliDate.today(tz=None)`, with `Asia/Kathmandu` when no zone is given. On Windows it
  needs the `tzdata` package for the zone database.

Converting a timestamp to a BS date is always explicit about the zone: pass a calendar date, or say
which zone the instant should be read in.

## Fiscal year

Nepal's fiscal year starts on Shrawan 1. The fiscal year that starts in BS 2080 is labelled
`2080/81` and ends on the last day of Ashad 2081. Every port's `fiscal_year` (or its idiomatic name)
gives the start, the end and that label.
